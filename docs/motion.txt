# Motion Design Master Playbook (v2)

Improved version of the earlier guides. It adds three things the first versions lacked:

1. **Verified library facts** from live web research (October 2026), with unverified items clearly marked.
2. **Measurements from your 8 reference videos** (cut counts, audio/visual sync, easing timing), with the method and limits stated.
3. **Decision tools**: how to pick effects, how to avoid repeating yourself, how to plan a video from brief to export.

Companion files: `motion-code-starters.md` (code and expressions) and `motion-project-templates.md` (fill-in templates).

## Part 1. How to Decide What Motion to Use

### 1.1 Decision flow

```
1. What is the video's job?
   Sell / Explain / Show a product / Build a brand / Entertain
        |
2. What is the tone? (pick ONE)
   Calm-premium | Friendly-playful | Techy-precise | Bold-energetic | Editorial-cultural
        |
3. What is the platform?
   Vertical social | Landscape web/YouTube | App Store / product page | Presentation
        |
4. Pick ONE signature technique (Section 1.2), then 2 supporting ones.
        |
5. Lock the motion language (Section 1.3) before animating anything.
```

### 1.2 Signature technique by job and tone

| Job and tone | Signature technique | Supporting | Seen in your references |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explain an app feature, friendly | **One object morphing through states** | Cursor, highlighter marks, soft glass | V2 |
| Product launch, techy | **Chaos-to-order story** (dark to light) | Line draw, radial fan, real UI | V1 |
| Tagline or mission, bold | **Kinetic type flow** (sentence scrolls as it builds) | Gradient text, counter punchline | V4 |
| Title or logo name, playful | **Per-letter shape-shifting** (short words only) | Motion blur, tracking animation | V5 |
| Educational reel, editorial | **Cutout objects as visual metaphors** | Serif/sans mix, ghost text, starbursts | V6 |
| Brand film, organic | **Recurring carrier shape** (mascot) | Dark/light alternation, floating UI cards | V7 |
| Design-resource reel | **Template + palette swatches** | Slow animated mesh gradient | V8 |
| Long promo with structure | **Chapter tabs** | Text cards alternating with footage | V3 |

### 1.3 Motion language (lock this per project)

Write these down before you open the animation software:

| Decision | Options | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Easing family** | Expo-out (premium), spring (app-like), back-out (playful), in-out (neutral) | Easing is the personality of the motion |
| **Shape language** | Rounded (friendly), angular (energetic), organic blobs (soft), geometric grid (technical) | Keeps morphs and icons coherent |
| **Transition type** | Morph, mask wipe, push, zoom-through, match cut, whip pan | Limit to 3 types per video |
| **Camera** | Locked, slow push, parallax, orbit, handheld | Camera movement sets the mood |
| **Type behavior** | Line mask-slide, word stagger, per-letter, blur-in | Type reveals carry most of the message |
| **Background** | Flat, mesh gradient, grid, footage, grain | Gradients: slow only |
| **Rhythm** | Fast and cut-heavy, medium, slow and flowing | Decides durations |

### 1.4 The anti-repetition system

1. Keep an **effect log** (template in `motion-project-templates.md`). Record signature technique, easing, shape language and transition for each project.
2. **Rule:** a new project must differ from the previous one in at least 3 of the 7 decisions in table 1.3.
3. **Budget per video:** 1 signature technique, 2 to 3 supporting, 3 transition types maximum, 1 background style.
4. **Test before adding an effect:** "If I remove this, does the message get weaker?" If no, remove it.
5. **Rotate sources**: study motion from film titles, UI design, print and typography, not only from other motion reels.

## Part 2. What Your Reference Videos Measure Out To

### 2.1 Method and limits

- **Cut detection:** ffmpeg scene-change detection (threshold 0.35).
- **Audio onsets:** spectral-flux peak detection on mono audio.
- **Visual events:** peaks in frame-to-frame pixel difference.
- **Alignment:** share of visual events that fall within 80 ms of an audio onset.
- **Limits:** these are automated estimates on WhatsApp-compressed files. Tempo estimates (BPM) from this method are unreliable on music with soft rhythm, so I do not rely on them. Alignment numbers are indicative, not exact. Treat them as hints on where to look.

### 2.2 Results

| Video | Length | Hard cuts | Cuts per 10 s | Audio track | Visual/audio alignment (<80 ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 AI launch | 31 s | 7 | 2.2 | Yes | 23% (13 visual events) |
| V2 App UI | 11 s | 0 | 0 | Yes | 67% (3 events) |
| V3 HowDo | 44 s | 3 | 0.7 | Yes | 27% (15 events) |
| V4 Kinetic text | 8 s | 0 | 0 | **Silent** | n/a |
| V5 Letters | 5 s | 0 | 0 | Yes | 100% (2 events) |
| V6 Web Rank | 19 s | 6 | 3.1 | Yes | 18% (11 events) |
| V7 Green brand | 47 s | 4 | 0.8 | Yes | 21% (14 events) |
| V8 Gradients | 25 s | 6 | 2.4 | **Silent** | n/a |

### 2.3 What to take from the numbers

- **Short, UI-focused pieces (V2, V5) sync tightly to audio.** The few strong visual moments land on sound hits. Sound design is part of the motion, not an afterthought.
- **Longer pieces (V1, V3, V6, V7) mostly do not hit-sync every move.** They use music as a bed with some key hits. Low alignment tells you that you do not need to sync everything. Sync the 3 to 5 moments that matter.
- **Two of the eight (V4, V8) have no audio.** They are likely made for silent autoplay feeds, so the motion itself has to carry the rhythm. If your video will be seen muted, design for that.
- **Cuts are rare.** The reference set averages about 1.4 hard cuts per 10 seconds, and three pieces have none. Most scene changes are made by morphing, wiping or zooming.
- **Vertical pieces cut more** (V6: 3.1 and V8: 2.4 per 10 s) than landscape brand pieces (V3: 0.7, V7: 0.8). This suggests vertical content is paced faster. This is a small sample of 8 videos, so treat it as a pattern to test, not a rule.

### 2.4 Frame-level observations

**V2 folder-to-checklist opening (first 3.4 s), measured at 30 fps by tracking the object's bounding box:**

| Time | What happens | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 to 0.3 s | Flat blue folder, cursor arrives from lower right, click, folder blurs into a frosted-glass version | Fast style swap on click |
| 0.3 to 1.2 s | Yellow note card rises out of the folder and the whole object grows steadily | About 0.9 s ease-out growth |
| 1.2 to 1.5 s | Object reaches its maximum height | Brief peak |
| 1.5 to 1.9 s | Object compresses and settles to a smaller, resting size | Settle after a small overshoot, about 0.3 to 0.4 s |
| 1.9 to 3.0 s | Card holds still while the hand cursor swirls away left with motion blur | Deliberate pause (about 1 s of near-stillness) |
| 3.0 to 3.4 s | Cursor returns, click, folder drops away, checklist expands | Second morph |

Takeaways: a **hold of about 1 second** between morphs makes the next one feel intentional. The overshoot-and-settle after growth is about 0.3 s. The cursor is blurred when moving fast.

**V5 text sequence (5.2 s):**
- The first word starts with **tight letter-spacing that opens up** over roughly the first second, then sits stable.
- The second word joins with a **small glyph flying in** to form "Creative & Smooth".
- Later, glyphs **scatter and regroup** into new word shapes with heavy motion blur.
- **Orientation:** the file has no rotation tag and its content is drawn sideways inside a portrait canvas, so it plays sideways on a phone. If you reuse this idea, create it upright.

## Part 3. Fundamentals, with Numbers

### 3.1 Easing quick table

| Use | Easing | cubic-bezier |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance (default) | Ease-out | `0.0, 0.0, 0.2, 1` |
| Exit | Ease-in | `0.4, 0.0, 1, 1` |
| Move across screen | In-out | `0.4, 0.0, 0.2, 1` |
| Premium reveal | Expo-out | `0.16, 1, 0.3, 1` |
| Soft deceleration | Quart-out | `0.25, 1, 0.5, 1` |
| Playful pop | Back-out | `0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1` |
| UI object settle | Spring (damping about 0.7, light overshoot) | physics, not bezier |

### 3.2 Durations at 30 fps

| Action | Seconds | Frames |
|---|---|---|
| Icon pop or toggle | 0.15 to 0.3 | 5 to 9 |
| Element entrance | 0.3 to 0.6 | 9 to 18 |
| Text line reveal | 0.4 to 0.8 | 12 to 24 |
| Morph between states | 0.4 to 0.9 | 12 to 27 |
| Scene transition | 0.4 to 0.8 | 12 to 24 |
| Logo reveal | 1.0 to 2.0 | 30 to 60 |
| Stagger step | 0.05 to 0.12 | 2 to 4 |
| Hold between morphs | 0.7 to 1.2 | 20 to 36 |
| Final logo hold | 1.5 to 3.0 | 45 to 90 |

The V2 measurement above supports the morph duration and the hold range. The other values are standard practice rather than measured from your files.

### 3.3 Reading time

About 3 words per second is comfortable. A headline of N words needs about N/3 seconds of full visibility after its animation completes, plus about 0.5 s.

### 3.4 Shape and icon rules

- One stroke weight, one corner radius family, one style (line, filled or duotone) per video.
- Animate meaning: bell rings, lock clicks, graph climbs, paper plane flies.
- Trim paths for line icons, scale-with-overshoot for filled icons, morph for state changes.
- Shape psychology: circle (friendly, complete), square (stable), triangle (direction, energy), blob (organic), line (connection).

### 3.5 Typography rules

- Two typefaces maximum. Change weight and italic for variety instead of adding fonts (V6 does this well).
- Per-letter animation only on 1 to 3 short words.
- Ghost text (large, low-contrast word behind) adds depth cheaply (V6, V7).
- Highlight key words with color, weight or a marker box (V4, V7).
- Safe zone: keep key text in the central 70% of vertical frames.

### 3.6 Color and light

- 3 to 5 colors. One accent reserved for what matters.
- Gradients move slowly (8 to 15 s loops). Add 2 to 5% grain to prevent banding.
- Glass look: backdrop blur, 1 px light edge, soft inner highlight, soft shadow.

## Part 4. Effects Catalogue with Use / Avoid Rules

### 4.1 Transitions

| Transition | Use when | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| Morph (object becomes next object) | Feature walkthroughs, app states | Shapes are unrelated |
| Match cut | Two scenes share a shape | Nothing in common |
| Shape or mask wipe | Brand shape can carry the move | Already used several wipes |
| Push or slide | Steps in a sequence | Emotional, slow content |
| Zoom-through | Overview to detail | Rapid cutting |
| Whip pan | Energetic social ads | Premium calm brands |
| Cross dissolve | Time passing, emotion | Fast promos |
| Cut on action | Fast pace | Rarely wrong |
| Glitch | Techy or gaming | Finance, wellness, luxury |
| Liquid or ink morph | Beauty, music, creative | Corporate data |
| Light leak or flash | Cinematic, events | App UI |

### 4.2 Reveals and text

Mask reveal (default), scale and fade (subtle), trim-path draw-on (lines, handwriting), stagger cascade (lists), blur-to-focus (cinematic), light sweep (logos, once only), typewriter (code and AI chat), scramble (tech, once), kinetic flow (taglines).

### 4.3 Backgrounds

Flat plus grain (safe), animated mesh gradient (modern SaaS, keep slow), aurora blobs (soft, AI), grid (technical), particles (sparse only), footage with dark overlay (human stories).

### 4.4 Overused effects (use rarely)

Glitch, heavy lens flare, constant bounce or elastic, spinning transitions, particles everywhere, neon on everything, extreme 3D extrusion, zoom punches every 2 seconds.

## Part 6. Workflow

1. **Brief** (template): goal, audience, platform, length, CTA, brand rules.
2. **Motion language** (table 1.3).
3. **Script**: short lines, about 3 words per second reading speed.
4. **Style frames**: 3 to 5 still key frames approved before animating.
5. **Storyboard and animatic**: rough timing with audio.
6. **Block**: key poses and positions only.
7. **Ease**: shape the curves in the graph editor.
8. **Secondary motion**: overlap, follow-through, blur.
9. **Sound design**: hits on the 3 to 5 key moments, ambient bed.
10. **Finish**: color, grain, safe-margin check.
11. **Review**: phone, muted, full-screen.
12. **Export**: per platform.

### Export cheat sheet

| Need | Format |
|---|---|
| Social and web | H.264 MP4 |
| Master or editing handoff | ProRes 422 or 4444 |
| Transparent | ProRes 4444 with alpha, or WebM with alpha |
| In-app animation | Lottie JSON or Rive |
| Vertical 9:16 | 1080 x 1920 |
| Landscape 16:9 | 1920 x 1080 or 3840 x 2160 |
| Square 1:1 | 1080 x 1080 |

App Store preview specs change often. Check the current Apple and Google requirements before exporting.

### Polish checklist

- [ ] Every entrance eased, exits faster than entrances
- [ ] One focal point at any moment
- [ ] Holds of 0.7 to 1.2 s between morphs
- [ ] Text visible long enough to read
- [ ] Safe margins respected
- [ ] 3 to 5 sound hits aligned to key visuals
- [ ] Palette and type consistent
- [ ] Muted version still communicates
- [ ] Tested on a phone
- [ ] Logo hold at least 1.5 s

## 1. Easing Reference (JavaScript)

```js
// All functions take t in [0,1] and return eased progress in [0,1] (back/elastic can overshoot)
export const ease = {
  linear:    t => t,
  outCubic:  t => 1 - Math.pow(1 - t, 3),
  outQuart:  t => 1 - Math.pow(1 - t, 4),
  outExpo:   t => (t === 1 ? 1 : 1 - Math.pow(2, -10 * t)),
  inCubic:   t => t * t * t,
  inOutCubic:t => (t < 0.5 ? 4 * t * t * t : 1 - Math.pow(-2 * t + 2, 3) / 2),
  outBack:   (t, s = 1.70158) => 1 + (s + 1) * Math.pow(t - 1, 3) + s * Math.pow(t - 1, 2),
};

// Map a frame range to eased progress
export const progress = (frame, start, end, fn = ease.outCubic) => {
  const t = Math.min(1, Math.max(0, (frame - start) / (end - start)));
  return fn(t);
};
```

## 2. After Effects Expressions

Paste into the property's expression field (Alt/Option-click the stopwatch).

### 2.1 Overshoot bounce after a keyframe
Works on scale or position after the last keyframe. Tune the three numbers at the top.

```js
amp = 0.1;     // overshoot strength (higher = bouncier)
freq = 2.0;    // oscillations per second
decay = 5.0;   // how quickly it settles (higher = faster)

n = 0;
if (numKeys > 0) {
  n = nearestKey(time).index;
  if (key(n).time > time) n--;
}
if (n == 0) {
  t = 0;
} else {
  t = time - key(n).time;
}
if (n > 0) {
  v = velocityAtTime(key(n).time - thisComp.frameDuration / 10);
  value + v * amp * Math.sin(freq * t * 2 * Math.PI) / Math.exp(decay * t);
} else {
  value;
}
```

### 2.2 Stagger by layer index (opacity or position offset)
Select many layers, apply to opacity. Each layer starts a little later than the one above.

```js
stepSec = 0.08;                       // delay between layers (about 2 to 3 frames at 30 fps)
start   = 1.0;                        // when the first layer begins
dur     = 0.5;                        // entrance duration
t0 = start + (index - 1) * stepSec;
easeOut(time, t0, t0 + dur, 0, 100);
```

### 2.3 Slide-up with ease (for position)
```js
start = 0.5; dur = 0.6; dist = 60;     // pixels to travel
y = easeOut(time, start, start + dur, dist, 0);
value + [0, y];
```

### 2.4 Curved cursor path (quadratic bezier)
Apply to the cursor layer's position. Never move a cursor in a straight line.

```js
p0 = [200, 800];     // start
p1 = [600, 700];     // control point (pulls the curve)
p2 = [900, 400];     // end
tStart = 1.0; tEnd = 2.2;

u = easeOut(time, tStart, tEnd, 0, 1);   // eased 0..1
a = (1 - u) * (1 - u);
b = 2 * (1 - u) * u;
c = u * u;
[ a*p0[0] + b*p1[0] + c*p2[0],
  a*p0[1] + b*p1[1] + c*p2[1] ];
```

### 2.5 Click pulse (scale dip) at a given time
```js
clickT = 2.2;                            // time of the click in seconds
d = time - clickT;
s = (d >= 0 && d < 0.2) ? 100 - 15 * Math.sin(Math.PI * d / 0.2) : 100;
[s, s];
```

### 2.6 Count-up number (Source Text)
```js
start = 1.0; dur = 1.2; from = 0; to = 10;
v = Math.round(easeOut(time, start, start + dur, from, to));
v + "x better";
```

### 2.7 Slow constant push-in (scale)
```js
dur = thisComp.duration;
s = linear(time, 0, dur, 100, 104);
[s, s];
```

### 2.8 Loop a keyframed animation
```js
loopOut("cycle");     // repeats the keyframed segment
```

### 2.9 Gentle float (loop-free)
```js
wiggle(0.5, 8);       // 0.5 times per second, 8 pixel amplitude. Apply to position.
```

### 2.10 Slow gradient drift (position of a blurred blob)
```js
loopTime = 12;                                     // seconds per loop
a = (time % loopTime) / loopTime * 2 * Math.PI;
value + [Math.cos(a) * 120, Math.sin(a) * 80];
```

Tip: shape the speed curve in the **Graph Editor** after applying keyframes. Ease-out entrances should start steep and end with a long gentle tail.

## 3. GSAP Timelines

No plugin required for these. They plan sequences well and can be screen-recorded to video.

### 3.1 Mask-style line reveal with stagger

```html
<div class="line"><span>Give your team</span></div>
<div class="line"><span>superpowers</span></div>

<style>
  .line { overflow: hidden; display: block; }       /* the mask */
  .line span { display: inline-block; transform: translateY(110%); }
</style>

<script src="https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/gsap@3/dist/gsap.min.js"></script>
<script>
  const tl = gsap.timeline({ defaults: { ease: "expo.out", duration: 0.7 } });
  tl.to(".line span", { y: 0, stagger: 0.1 })
    .to({}, { duration: 1.2 })                       // hold so the viewer can read
    .to(".line span", { y: "-110%", ease: "power2.in", duration: 0.4, stagger: 0.06 });
</script>
```

### 3.2 Morph hold pattern (state A to B, pause, B to C)

```js
const tl = gsap.timeline();
tl.to("#card", { scale: 1.15, duration: 0.9, ease: "power3.out" })   // grow
  .to("#card", { scale: 1.0,  duration: 0.35, ease: "power2.inOut" }) // settle
  .to({}, { duration: 1.0 })                                          // hold about 1 s
  .to("#card", { height: 320, borderRadius: 24, duration: 0.6, ease: "power3.inOut" });
```

### 3.3 Icon pop with overshoot and draw-on line
```js
gsap.fromTo(".icon", { scale: 0 }, { scale: 1, duration: 0.5, ease: "back.out(1.7)" });

// SVG path draw-on (works without plugins using stroke-dasharray)
const path = document.querySelector("#underline");
const len = path.getTotalLength();
gsap.set(path, { strokeDasharray: len, strokeDashoffset: len });
gsap.to(path, { strokeDashoffset: 0, duration: 0.8, ease: "power2.out" });
```

### 3.4 Slowly drifting gradient blobs
```js
gsap.to(".blob-1", { x: 120, y: -60, duration: 12, ease: "sine.inOut", repeat: -1, yoyo: true });
gsap.to(".blob-2", { x: -100, y: 80, duration: 15, ease: "sine.inOut", repeat: -1, yoyo: true });
```
Use large blurred shapes (`filter: blur(80px)`) and a grain overlay.

### 3.5 Recording a GSAP animation for video
Screen-record the browser at 60 fps (or use a headless capture tool) and import into your editor. Keep animations time-based so frame rates do not change speed. Check licensing terms for any GSAP plugin you use commercially.

## 4. Remotion Compositions

Remotion renders React components frame by frame. These use its core APIs: `useCurrentFrame`, `useVideoConfig`, `interpolate`, `spring`, `Easing`, `Sequence`, `AbsoluteFill`.

### 4.1 Project registration (`src/Root.tsx`)

```tsx
import { Composition } from "remotion";
import { Tagline } from "./Tagline";

export const RemotionRoot = () => (
  <Composition
    id="Tagline"
    component={Tagline}
    durationInFrames={240}   // 8 s at 30 fps
    fps={30}
    width={1920}
    height={1080}
  />
);
```

### 4.2 Kinetic tagline with mask reveal, hold and exit (`src/Tagline.tsx`)

```tsx
import { AbsoluteFill, Easing, interpolate, useCurrentFrame } from "remotion";

const words = ["Give", "your", "team", "superpowers"];

const Word: React.FC<{ text: string; index: number }> = ({ text, index }) => {
  const frame = useCurrentFrame();
  const start = 10 + index * 4;           // stagger: 4 frames per word
  const inEnd = start + 18;               // 18 frames entrance
  const outStart = 150 + index * 2;       // hold, then exit
  const outEnd = outStart + 12;

  const enter = interpolate(frame, [start, inEnd], [110, 0], {
    easing: Easing.out(Easing.exp),
    extrapolateLeft: "clamp",
    extrapolateRight: "clamp",
  });
  const exit = interpolate(frame, [outStart, outEnd], [0, -110], {
    easing: Easing.in(Easing.cubic),
    extrapolateLeft: "clamp",
    extrapolateRight: "clamp",
  });

  return (
    <span style={{ display: "inline-block", overflow: "hidden", marginRight: 24 }}>
      <span style={{ display: "inline-block", transform: `translateY(${enter + exit}%)` }}>
        {text}
      </span>
    </span>
  );
};

export const Tagline: React.FC = () => (
  <AbsoluteFill
    style={{
      background: "#f7f6f8",
      justifyContent: "center",

      fontSize: 120,
      fontWeight: 700,
      color: "#1b1b1f",
    }}
  >
    <div>
      {words.map((w, i) => (
        <Word key={w} text={w} index={i} />
      ))}
    </div>
  </AbsoluteFill>
);
```

### 4.3 Spring pop for icons or cards

```tsx
import { spring, useCurrentFrame, useVideoConfig } from "remotion";

export const PopCard: React.FC<{ delay?: number }> = ({ delay = 0 }) => {
  const frame = useCurrentFrame();
  const { fps } = useVideoConfig();

  const s = spring({
    frame: frame - delay,
    fps,
    config: { damping: 12, stiffness: 120 },   // lower damping = more bounce
  });

  return (
    <div
      style={{
        width: 400, height: 240, borderRadius: 32, background: "#fff",
        boxShadow: "0 30px 60px rgba(0,0,0,.12)",
        transform: `scale(${s})`,
        opacity: Math.min(1, s * 2),
      }}
    />
  );
};
```

## 2. Video-by-Video Breakdown

### V1: AI product launch (31 s, 16:9)

**What happens (from the sampled frames):**
1. Black screen with scattered symbols (`/`, `<`, `>`, `*`) and thin guide lines around the words "too many signals". Chaos.

## 5. What Makes These Feel Professional

1. **Continuous motion over cuts.** Most references transform one thing into the next instead of cutting. Plan your transitions as part of the design, not as separate effects.
2. **One focal point at a time.** Even in the busy scenes (V3, V7), only one thing is the star.
3. **A strict palette per video.** V6 is purple, black and white. V7 is green, lime and cream. V8 is one gradient family per scene.
4. **Typography does real work.** Weight changes, highlighted words, ghost text and italic serif contrast carry emphasis without extra effects.
5. **Rest between busy moments.** White cards or footage after dense graphics gives the eye a break.
6. **Rounded, soft geometry** in app-style videos (V2, V4, V7). Sharp, angular geometry only in V6's starbursts for energy.
7. **Light interactivity cues** (cursor, click ripple, toggle) make a video feel like a real product.
8. **A strong end card.** V6, V7 and V8 all finish with a clear, calm closing frame.

## 6. Quick Production Notes

- **Soft gradients:** build with large blurred shapes (fractal noise or Gaussian blur) and slow position keyframes. Add grain on top.
- **Glass:** blurred backdrop, 1 px light border, soft inner highlight, subtle drop shadow.
- **Cursor:** a vector hand/pointer with its own scale-down on click and a ripple.
- **Highlighter strokes:** a shape layer with a semi-transparent yellow stroke and trim paths, with slight irregular thickness.
- **Per-letter animation:** in After Effects use the Text Animator with Range Selector, or split to shapes for custom paths. In code, SplitText-style libraries do the same.
- **Mascot/carrier shape:** build as a shape layer with parametric controls (scale, rotation, petals), so the same rig can appear in many scenes.
- **Vertical videos (V5, V6, V8):** keep key text in the middle 70% of the frame to avoid platform UI.
